## Fixture structure

Each of the ten registry detectors ([Detector Registry](/docs/detectors)) ships with exactly
four small, purposeful Solidity fixtures in `contracts/benchmarks/`, rather than a large pile
of near-duplicate examples padded to hit a count:

1. **Vulnerable** — triggers the detector; the minimal contract that demonstrates the
   fund-lock condition the detector exists to catch.
2. **Corrected** — the same contract with the specific issue fixed and nothing else changed,
   so the detector must *not* fire on it.
3. **Safe negative control** — an unrelated, legitimately safe contract that looks nothing
   like the vulnerable pattern; guards against a detector that fires too broadly.
4. **False-positive test** — a contract that is superficially similar to the vulnerable
   pattern (same function names, similar structure) but is not actually vulnerable; this is
   the fixture most likely to catch an overly aggressive detector.

That's 40 fixture contracts total (10 × 4), each with a `ground-truth.json` recording the
expected finding (or explicitly, the expected absence of one), the taxonomy category, and a
short explanation a reviewer can check by hand.

## Foundry tests

Where a fund-lock condition can be demonstrated executably (for example, a withdrawal that
reverts under every reachable state), the fixture also has a Foundry test that proves it —
not just a static-analysis claim. This is the same local-only Foundry/Anvil environment the
analyzer's validation adapter uses; benchmark fixtures and live analysis validation share one
execution path rather than two that could quietly disagree.

## Scoring

The benchmark runner (`services/analyzer` — see [Architecture](/docs/architecture)) executes
the full detector registry against every fixture and reports:

- **Precision** — of everything FLARE flagged, how much was a real, ground-truth-confirmed
  finding.
- **Recall** — of everything that should have been flagged, how much FLARE actually caught.
- **False-positive rate** on the dedicated false-positive fixtures specifically, reported
  separately from the general precision figure since that's the number most likely to be
  inflated by an easy benchmark.

Results are written to `evaluation/` as a versioned report, and the dashboard's "Recent
benchmark result" card reads the same file — it is never a hand-typed number.

## What this benchmark does not claim

Forty fixtures across ten detectors is enough to check that each detector's specific,
documented condition behaves correctly — it is not a claim that FLARE has been validated
against the full diversity of real-world Solidity. See [Limitations](/docs/limitations) for
what a passing benchmark here does and doesn't tell you about an arbitrary contract in the
wild.
