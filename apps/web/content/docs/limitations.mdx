This page is updated as the implementation changes — a limitation removed here means the
corresponding milestone actually shipped, checked against real tests, not just described. The
canonical, most current version always lives at
[`docs/LIMITATIONS.md`](https://github.com/KartikBuilds/FLARE/blob/main/docs/LIMITATIONS.md)
in the repository.

## Chain support

Only the EVM/Solidity analyzer is implemented. The Solana/Rust adapter referenced in
[Architecture](/docs/architecture) is an interface-only stub — it defines the shape a future
implementation would take and is not connected to any working analysis. Any Solana-related
case study on this site (Lido on Solana) is documentation only; it cannot be reproduced by
running the FLARE engine.

## No trained machine-learning model

FLARE does not ship a trained graph neural network or any other trained ML detection model.
Detector logic is deterministic, hand-specified rules over Slither's static-analysis output —
see [Detector Registry](/docs/detectors). If a GNN or other learned model is added in the
future, this page will say so only once it has a documented training process and a reported
evaluation, not before.

## AI assistance is optional and secondary

When an AI provider is configured, it may explain existing deterministic findings, summarize
architecture, or suggest a remediation checklist. It cannot create a confirmed finding,
fabricate a source location, or claim a validation result that didn't happen. The full
pipeline — intake through risk scoring — runs identically with AI disabled, which is also the
default.

## Detector registry scope

The registry ships ten detectors, two per taxonomy category, deliberately smaller than an
exhaustive list — see [Detector Registry](/docs/detectors) for why quality per detector was
prioritized over count, and [Benchmark Plan](/docs/benchmark) for exactly how each one is
scored. A contract free of findings from these ten detectors is not thereby proven free of
every fund-lock risk; it means these ten specific, documented conditions were checked for and
not found.

## Case-study data

Five incidents are documented in [Case Studies](/docs/case-studies). One — "Perfect
Finance" (2023) — could not be independently corroborated in public reporting as of this
research and is explicitly marked unverified rather than described as fact. Dollar figures
for verified incidents are approximate where they depend on which historical token price was
used for the conversion (most notably Parity, 2017); we report the underlying token amount as
the primary verified fact.

## Benchmark scope

Forty fixture contracts across ten detectors validate that each detector's specific,
documented condition behaves as intended (including on adversarial "looks similar but isn't"
fixtures). This is not a claim of validation against the full diversity of real-world
Solidity, and a passing benchmark result should be read as "these conditions are checked
correctly," not "this tool finds every fund-lock in arbitrary code."

## What "verified" via Foundry means

Where the validation adapter confirms a finding through a local, offline Foundry/Anvil run,
that means a concrete proof-of-concept executed against the compiled contract and produced
the predicted outcome — not that the finding was checked against a live or forked mainnet
state, and never against a public network.
